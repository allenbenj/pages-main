# Records request checklist

Use with `records-requests-subpoenas`. Answer every item. Mark an item "not applicable" with a reason rather than leaving it blank. Every legal standard named below is a question to verify for the governing jurisdiction, not a statement of law.

## A. Subpoena or court-ordered production

1. **Authority and jurisdiction.** The rule or statute that authorizes the request in this kind of case; the issuing court's power over the holder; whether an out-of-state holder requires a domestication or interstate procedure.
2. **Service.** Who may serve, how (personal, certified mail, registered agent, provider law-enforcement portal), and proof of service.
3. **Standard the court will apply.** For pretrial production in criminal cases, many courts ask whether the records are relevant, evidentiary, not otherwise reasonably obtainable, needed to prepare, and sought in good faith rather than as a general search. *United States v. Nixon*, 418 U.S. 683 (1974), is the usual federal starting point; it is candidate authority until verified and may not control in a state court.
4. **Specificity.** Name each category, account or identifier, and date range. "All records" is a defect.
5. **Relevance and materiality.** Each category ties to a named element, defense, or impeachment point, with the record facts showing why.
6. **Overbreadth and burden.** The date range and categories are the narrowest that answer the question; volume and format are reasonable; whether the court may shift production cost.
7. **Privilege and work product.** Nothing privileged is requested; a plan exists for inadvertent production.
8. **Privacy and statutory limits.** Content versus subscriber or log data; statutes that bar or condition disclosure to private parties; notice to the subject; sealing and redaction.
9. **Protective order.** Whether to propose one, and its terms (use limits, attorneys'-eyes-only tier, return or destruction).
10. **Return date and place.** A reasonable time; production to the clerk or to counsel as the rule requires; extension terms.
11. **Foundation.** Ask for the custodian certification or declaration the evidence rules require so the records can be authenticated without a live witness where allowed (`evidence-matrix-admissibility`).

## B. Likely objections and answers

| Objection | Answer to prepare |
|---|---|
| Overbroad or a fishing expedition | Category-by-category link to facts at issue; narrowed date range. |
| Unduly burdensome | Volume estimate; format flexibility; cost-sharing offer. |
| Privacy or statutory bar | The specific exception or court-order route; protective order; content excluded if barred. |
| Not relevant or not material | The element or impeachment point and the record facts behind it. |
| Obtainable elsewhere | What was tried, when, and why it failed. |
| Untimely | The rule's timing and the reason for the date. |

## C. Public-records requests

- Identify the governing public-records law and the correct custodian; cite the statute in the request.
- Describe the records specifically (type, date range, names, incident or case numbers) so the custodian can locate them.
- Ask for electronic copies and state the preferred format.
- Ask for a fee estimate before large production, and whether fees can be waived or reduced.
- Record the statutory response time, if any, and calendar the follow-up.
- Ask that any denial or redaction state the exemption relied on, and note the appeal or enforcement route.
- Keep a copy of the request, proof of delivery, and every response.

## D. Planning by backcasting

1. State the end state: the records in hand, authenticated, and usable at the hearing or trial.
2. State the present: what exists, who holds it, what has been refused.
3. List the conditions that must be true at the end (authority, service, order, custodian certification, time before the hearing).
4. Work backward from the end date to set each step's latest date.
5. Turn the steps into the action list and the open-items entries.
