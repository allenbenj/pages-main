# Case theory worksheet

Placeholders are illustrative. Fill them only from the record.

## Theory statement

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Audience | Judge, jury, mediator, insurer, or opposing counsel |
| Decision and standard | What it decides, the standard, and its verified source (or UNVERIFIED) |
| Theory (one sentence) | Who did what, why the law treats it as it does, and why the remedy follows |
| Elements or claims served | Each with its source |

Test: can a reader state the theory back in one breath, and does each element have a record fact beneath it?

## Theme table

| Theme | Why it fits this audience | Supporting facts | Source and pinpoint | R10 class | Elements served |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Limit to three themes. A theme with no record fact under it is dropped.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Narrative checks

- Each factual sentence cites a source and pinpoint (K6).
- Each accusation against a named official ties to cited record evidence (K6).
- Working labels are converted to attribution in court-facing text (K12).
- No framework label, score, or likelihood figure remains (K7).
- Language suits the audience; legal terms are explained for lay readers.
