# Court document template

Version: 0.2.1. Structure for motions, briefs, and memoranda. Adapt headings to the court's rules and the document type. Verify format requirements before filing (see `corrective-playbook-gate/references/pre-delivery-checklist.md` Stage D). Keep this template's guidance notes out of the filed paper.

```
[CAPTION: court, county, file number, parties, document title — checked against the court file]

INTRODUCTION
  One paragraph: who asks for what relief, on what legal basis, and why the court should grant it.
  State the strongest dispositive point first.

PROCEDURAL POSTURE
  Relevant filings, orders, and dates, each with a record cite.
  State each disposition with the document's own verb (ordered, recommended, granted, denied, pending).

FACTUAL BACKGROUND
  Chronological. Every sentence cites a record source by ID and pinpoint.
  Attribute contested facts ("The affidavit states…", "The State contends…").
  Include adverse facts that the court will need.

LEGAL STANDARD
  The governing statute, rule, or test quoted verbatim with pinpoint.
  Who bears the burden, and the standard of proof or review, with source.

ARGUMENT
  I. [Issue heading stated as a conclusion]
     Rule:        quoted and cited (reliance-approved authority only).
     Application: specific supported facts matched to each element.
     Counterargument: the strongest opposing position, stated fairly, and the answer.
     Conclusion:  what the court should decide on this issue.
  II. [Next issue, in issue-tree order: threshold, dispositive, then remaining]

REQUESTED RELIEF
  Numbered requests, each matching a paragraph of the proposed order.
  Narrower alternative relief where appropriate.

CONCLUSION
  One or two sentences restating the requested relief.

SIGNATURE BLOCK / VERIFICATION OR AFFIDAVIT (if required) / CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
  Placeholders only until a real person signs, swears, or serves. Never invent them.

EXHIBIT INDEX
  Exhibit label, description, source ID and path, foundation and admissibility basis.
```

## Writing rules

- Plain, active English. Short sentences. One idea per paragraph.
- Third-person professional voice. No rhetorical questions, invective, or speculation about motive without record support.
- Quote exactly. Mark alterations with brackets and omissions with ellipses.
- No framework labels (IRAC, Toulmin), model tags, prompts, scores, or review notes in the filed paper.
