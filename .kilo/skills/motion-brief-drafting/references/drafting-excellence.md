# Drafting Excellence — Additional Guidance

Use this reference when the basic template and rules are not enough to produce clear, persuasive, accurate prose. Load it when drafting an introduction, a fact section, or when the user asks for stronger writing.

## Openings That Work
A strong introduction does three things in one short paragraph:
1. Names the relief sought.
2. States the single strongest legal reason it should be granted.
3. Points to the key record fact or controlling authority.

**Example (suppression motion):**
"The Court should suppress the statements. The officers continued questioning after the defendant unambiguously invoked his right to counsel. The recording at 14:22 shows the invocation; the officers’ next questions appear at 14:24. Under *Edwards v. Arizona*, that sequence requires suppression."

**Example (research note opening):**
"Question: Whether the affidavit’s statement that the defendant ‘fled the scene’ is supported by the record. The body-worn camera shows the defendant walking away at a normal pace after the officers said ‘you’re free to go.’"

## Fact Sections
- Chronological unless a topical order better serves a single issue.
- Every sentence ends with a source citation in the form the matter uses (e.g., (Aff. ¶ 4); (Tr. 42:15–18)).
- Attribute contested facts: "The State’s affidavit states…" or "Officer Jones testified that…".
- Include the adverse facts the court will see. Burying them is worse than addressing them.

## Argument Transitions
After stating the rule, move directly to application:
"The rule requires X. Here, the record shows Y. Therefore Z."

When answering a counterargument:
"The State may argue that [fair statement of their point]. That argument fails because [record fact + authority]."

## Notes vs. Filings
| Feature | Research/Strategy Note | Court Filing |
|---------|------------------------|--------------|
| Audience | Internal / next skill | Court |
| Candor | High; list gaps openly | Professional; still accurate |
| Labels | May use "Inference:", "Gap:" | Avoid analytic labels |
| Length | As long as useful | Concise; local rules |
| Tone | Direct | Formal but plain |

## Common Fixes
- Replace "It is clear that" with the fact or rule that makes it clear.
- Replace long quotations with a short quote + pinpoint + paraphrase of the rest.
- Break any sentence longer than ~25 words.
- One topic per paragraph.

## Handoff Checklist (for notes that will become filings)
- [ ] Every material fact has a source.
- [ ] Controlling rules are quoted or marked UNVERIFIED.
- [ ] Element-by-element application is written.
- [ ] Adverse facts are listed.
- [ ] Recommended next skill and inputs are stated.
- [ ] Forensic-frame check done if medical/DNA/consent topics appear.
