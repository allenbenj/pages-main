# Defense-framing deliverable contracts

## Short attorney note (typically 500–1200 words)
**Subject; decision needed; position:** single focused paragraph. **Record:** only decisive facts, with source IDs/pins and contrary facts. **Analysis:** legal rule (verified or marked), materiality and causal logic. **Opposing argument:** the strongest fair version and specific answer. **Recommended action:** definite motion, witness question, discovery or preservation. **Verification needed:** only outstanding facts/authorities; not generic caveats.

## Long memorandum
To / from / date / confidentiality and procedural posture; question presented; short answer; supported chronology; governing law (authority ledger + dates); analysis with issue headings and developed IRAC/CREAC, contradictions with attribution, prejudice and remedy; competing analysis; conclusion and instructions; private appendix for open proof gaps. Do not put internal risk rubric into the deliverable.

## Motion argument section
A forceful topic sentence containing the *specific legal question*; fully written factual application with source pins; adverse fact and rebuttal; exact holding/standard from verified primary source; requested finding and operative remedy. Integrate with `motion-brief-drafting` for a finished motion and proposed order.

## Public narrative / hearing opening
Begin with one objective contradiction or documented decision that matters, not a categorical accusation. State the State's account fairly, demonstrate the central tension with traceable evidence, and explain the bounded consequence. Avoid legal claims about the accused beyond the record and verdict posture.

## Trial cross / hearing note
Fact to establish; exact exhibit or prior statement; foundation questions; sequenced short leading questions (where procedurally permitted); anticipated evasions; impeachment source; follow-up and preservation objection. Questions are prompts for licensed counsel, not facts in evidence.

## Mandatory drafting completeness tests
1. The prose, not the outline, occupies most of the requested artifact.
2. The reader can identify the concrete relief or next step without reading an appendix.
3. No naked conclusion of intentional wrongdoing appears without the necessary proof analysis.
4. Every material claim has attribution and a source location; opposing evidence is included.
5. No sentence begins with "And" in final deliverable prose.
6. Internal model evaluation labels do not leak into client/court-facing material.
7. Any part not verified is conspicuously separated; do not silently present it as verified.
