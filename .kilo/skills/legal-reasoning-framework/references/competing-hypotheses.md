# Competing hypotheses and diagnosticity

Use this worksheet when the analysis must compare the State's account, the defense account, and any grounded alternative. It is a reasoning aid, not a finding.

## Accounts

| Account | Claim | Source pins |
|---|---|---|
| State | What the State says happened, and why. | Each factual sentence with a pin. |
| Defense | What the defense says happened, and why. | Each factual sentence with a pin. |
| Alternative 1 | A grounded alternative explanation. | The record facts that make it plausible. |
| Alternative 2 | Another grounded alternative, if any. | The record facts that make it plausible. |

Do not invent an alternative that the record does not support. Do not omit a grounded alternative because it is inconvenient.

## Evidence matrix

For each material item:

| Item | For State | For defense | For alternative | Neutral | Reliability | Diagnosticity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Item and pin] | Yes/No/Weak | Yes/No/Weak | Yes/No/Weak | Yes/No | High/Medium/Low with reason | Which accounts does it distinguish? |

## Two different questions

- **Logical consistency.** The item does not contradict the account. That is a low bar; many items are consistent with several accounts.
- **Discriminatory force (diagnosticity).** The item is more likely under one account than another. That is what distinguishes the accounts.

A datum consistent with both accounts proves neither. Do not write "consistent with" as if it meant "proves." Do not force false symmetry where the evidence differs materially; say which account the evidence favors and why.

## Reliability

Assess the source, the chain, the method, and the witness. A weak item can be consistent with an account without adding discriminatory force. State the reason, not a label.

## Missing evidence

Identify the item, test, interview, or record that would distinguish the accounts. For each, state:

- what is missing;
- why it would be diagnostic;
- whether the step was ordinary practice or a required duty, with a verified source or `UNVERIFIED`;
- the effect its absence has on the comparison;
- the next action.

Use `references/investigative-standards-index.md` for the standard or practice, and `discovery-brady-giglio` for the request.

## Numeric limits

- In local working notes, a 1–10 score may supplement the words.
- In a final document, no numeric likelihood, probability, or score. State the strength and the reasons in words (K7).
- Do not convert a Bayesian working estimate into a guilt probability.

## Output

- The account table.
- The evidence matrix with for/against/neutral and reliability.
- A diagnosticity narrative: which items distinguish the accounts, and which do not.
- The missing evidence and the next action.
- The strongest adverse point and the response.
- A statement of what remains unresolved.
