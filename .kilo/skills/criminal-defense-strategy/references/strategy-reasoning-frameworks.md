# Criminal Defense Strategy — Reasoning Frameworks Reference

This reference establishes the analytical disciplines required when evaluating criminal defense theories, evidentiary sufficiency, and legal challenges under `criminal-defense-strategy`.

---

## 1. Core Mandate: Analytical Substance Over Worksheets

Frameworks exist to test and sharpen legal reasoning, not to substitute for it. An agent or strategist using these tools must never terminate an assignment in a blank matrix, checklist, or mechanical diagram. Every analysis must culminate in draft-ready legal prose that articulates:
1. The precise defense theory and the legal standard governing relief.
2. The specific evidentiary record supporting each factual premise.
3. The opposing inference and the State's strongest anticipated counterargument.
4. The reasoned rebuttal grounded in record evidence and governing authority.
5. The concrete recommendation: pursue, investigate, preserve, or reject.

---

## 2. The Four Analytical Frameworks

### 2.1. IRAC / CREAC (Issue-Driven Legal Argument)

Use IRAC/CREAC to structure the substantive legal evaluation of each discrete defense issue or claim:
- **Issue / Conclusion:** State the precise legal question and the defense contention (e.g., whether the State's circumstantial evidence of departure is legally sufficient to establish the element of taking).
- **Rule:** Set forth the controlling constitutional provision, statute, procedural rule, or governing precedent, including the specific burden of proof and standard of review applicable at the current procedural stage.
- **Explanation:** Explain the legal standard through relevant appellate precedent, showing what factual thresholds courts have required to grant or deny relief.
- **Application:** Apply the rule directly to the concrete facts of the record. Walk through the evidence item by item, distinguishing record facts from witness assertions, inferences, and unsupported allegations.
- **Counteranalysis & Rebuttal:** Confront the prosecution's strongest argument directly. Demonstrate why the State's theory fails under the governing standard.
- **Conclusion / Remedy:** State the specific legal consequence and requested relief (e.g., dismissal of count, suppression of physical evidence, disclosure of unredacted recordings, or entry of an order compelling identification of theories).

### 2.2. Toulmin Argumentation Structure

Use the Toulmin model to expose assumptions, vulnerabilities, and evidentiary gaps in both prosecution and defense arguments:
- **Claim:** The specific proposition to be established (e.g., "The State's proof does not identify the physical container in which property was allegedly removed").
- **Grounds (Evidence):** The specific, verified record facts supporting the claim (e.g., Chen states a crate labeled copper fittings and says Chen did not see inside it; Patel states an unmarked canvas bag and says Patel did not see a crate; the charging sheet names a storeroom inventory as Exhibit S-4 and states it is not included in the pack, so its contents are unknown). Ground only what a source says. Never supply the contents or collection history of a document the pack does not contain.
- **Warrant:** The underlying principle or logical bridge connecting the grounds to the claim. Treat the warrant as a candidate until its backing is verified (e.g., a candidate warrant: when two witnesses describe different containers and the relationship between their observations is unresolved, the State's proof of the container may be incomplete; whether that matters depends on the elements and standards verified for the jurisdiction).
- **Backing:** Precedent, statutory requirements, or accepted investigative practices supporting the warrant. Mark each as verified, `research_pending`, or `unverified`, and never present an unverified rule as settled.
- **Qualifier:** The degree of certainty justified by the current record (e.g., "contingent on production of S-4," "unverified on current packet").
- **Rebuttal:** Anticipated objections and conditions that would defeat the claim (e.g., "If an inventory is later produced and it matches the crate, the container challenge is weakened"; the State may also contend the two observations are compatible).

### 2.3. Causal-Chain Analysis

Use causal-chain analysis to trace the sequence of events, police actions, and evidentiary handling:
- **Chronology Mapping:** Place every event, observation, and police intervention on a strict timeline, noting exact timestamps and clock-synchronization status.
- **Chain of Custody & Preservation:** Trace physical and digital evidence from initial observation through collection, transport, testing, and storage. Identify every gap, delay, or unperformed standard procedure.
- **But-For Causation vs. Attenuation:** In constitutional challenges (Fourth, Fifth, Sixth Amendments), determine whether the challenged evidence is a direct causal product of an unlawful stop, search, or interrogation, or whether lawful independent sources, inevitable discovery, or intervening attenuation sever the chain.

### 2.4. Issue-Tree Decomposition

Use issue trees to deconstruct charged offenses into irreducible legal elements and decision paths:
- **Offense Breakdown:** Break each charged count into its statutory elements (e.g., Mens Rea, Actus Reus, Attendant Circumstances, Result/Causation).
- **Branching Decision Points:** For each element, establish:
  1. Does the State possess direct evidence, circumstantial evidence, or merely an uncorroborated allegation?
  2. Is the evidence admissible under governing rules of evidence?
  3. Does a plausible alternative innocent hypothesis explain the record?
  4. What pretrial motion or trial defense directly attacks this branch?
- **Failure-of-Proof vs. Affirmative Defense:** Prioritize attacks on the State's failure to meet its prima facie burden beyond a reasonable doubt before considering affirmative defenses that shift burdens or concede acts.

## 3. Integrating Frameworks into Strategy Work Product

When drafting a strategy packet or analytical memorandum:
1. **Never reproduce framework worksheets as the deliverable.** The worksheets are internal scaffolding; the deliverable is structured argument prose.
2. **Every factual assertion must cite a proposition or evidence ID.** Unattributed facts or speculative narratives are prohibited.
3. **Every legal argument record must be self-contained.** Downstream agents (such as `defense-writing-studio` and `motion-brief-drafting`) must be able to consume the packet directly without re-interviewing the strategist or guessing the rationale.
