# Civil Rule 26 expert review

Use this reference only in a civil case whose governing rules actually incorporate Rule 26 or a verified state analogue.

## Candidate authority

- `FED-R-CIV-P-26` — Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2). Status in the kit ledger: not yet seeded; record it as `UNVERIFIED` until the rule text and the scheduling order are read.
- A state analogue must be identified and verified separately. Do not assume the federal rule applies in state court.

## Retained experts

For a retained expert who must provide a written report, check:

- a complete statement of all opinions and the basis and reasons for them;
- the facts or data considered;
- the exhibits that will be used;
- the witness's qualifications, including publications from the preceding ten years;
- the compensation;
- the cases in which the witness testified in the preceding four years.

Verify the current rule text, the applicable subdivision, and any local rule or scheduling order that changes the requirements.

## Non-retained experts

A non-retained expert may be disclosed without a full written report, but the disclosure must still identify the subject matter and the summary of the opinion. Do not treat a non-retained expert as exempt from all disclosure.

## Timing and supplementation

- Identify the scheduling-order deadline and the trigger.
- Identify the supplementation duty and any expert-discovery cutoff.
- A late report or a changed opinion may require supplementation, a continuance, or exclusion; verify the remedy.

## Consulting experts

- A consulting expert may be protected from disclosure in some circumstances.
- The protection is not automatic and may be lost when the expert becomes a testifying expert or when the party relies on the consulting opinion.
- Identify the verified rule and the specific trigger before producing or withholding anything.

## Common errors

1. Applying Rule 26 in an NC criminal case.
2. Treating a signature or compensation statement as a complete report.
3. Treating a non-retained expert as having no disclosure obligation.
4. Assuming the consulting protection survives a decision to testify.
5. Ignoring the scheduling order and local rules.
6. Treating a state analogue as identical to the federal rule without reading it.

A civil Rule 26 matrix with the required report element, the disclosed status, the source pin, the deficiency, the remedy, and the State's or opposing party's best response.
