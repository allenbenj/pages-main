# Proposition levels

Classify every claim before evaluating it. The level determines what support is required and what the claim may not say.

| Level | Question | Minimum support | Prohibited move |
|---|---|---|---|
| `source` | What did the laboratory or expert report? | The exact report language and a source pin. | Treating the report as proof of an act or offense. |
| `activity` | What happened, who did it, or how was the item deposited? | A qualified expert opinion or other evidence that addresses the activity, with its limits. | Inflating a source association into an activity conclusion. |
| `offense` | Did the legal elements occur? | Evidence and legal analysis beyond the scientific source. | Treating a laboratory result as an element or a verdict. |
| `other` | A defined intermediate proposition. | A stated basis and a pin. | Leaving the level undefined. |

## Source level

The report says what it says. Quote it. State the method, the result, the reported statistic, and the limits. A source-level association is not an identification of a person and is not proof of an act.

Example: "The profile is consistent with the reference sample from Sample A."

## Activity level

The claim moves from the result to what happened. It needs a qualified opinion that addresses transfer, secondary transfer, shedding, persistence, timing, or the sampling site. If the record does not contain that opinion, the inference is `unresolved`; state the question and do not present the activity as established.

Example of an unsupported leap: "The DNA proves Sample A was present during the offense."

## Offense level

The claim moves from activity to the legal elements. It needs evidence and legal analysis beyond the scientific source. A laboratory report cannot carry an offense alone, and an expert cannot testify to the legal conclusion.

Example of an unsupported leap: "The DNA proves the offense occurred."

## Other

Use `other` only for a defined intermediate proposition. State the proposition and its basis. Do not use `other` to avoid classifying an activity or offense claim.

## Checks

- Does the claim use the report's language or a paraphrase? If a paraphrase, is the difference material?
- Does the claim move from source to activity or offense without a qualified basis?
- Does the claim use "consistent with" as if it were "proves" or "matches uniquely"?
- Does the claim treat a statistic as a guilt probability?
- Does the claim state a limit and then ignore it?
